I was born in Baghdad, Iraq, and was soon evacuated by the Red Cross to Sweden. When I was 9, my family moved again to the UK. Growing up I was the weakest of my friends, so as soon as I could afford a gym membership I began pursuing lifting obsessively and eventually made it to nationals coaching myself. I was training increasingly hard for little in return, and I wanted to pursue something with more purpose and impact. A family member's close call with cancer made me appreciate how transformative cures can be, and I decided making medicines would be the most important thing I could do in my life.

Cambridge gave me incredible access and the opportunity to speak with leaders in my field of interest. These conversations led to me doing research making inducible protein aggregation models at Harvard, and using protein aggregation for drug delivery depots at Cambridge.

At 19, I was disappointed that we hadn't succeeded in making disease-modifying therapeutics for many neurodegenerative diseases, and wanted to try to do it myself. I raised $15k from angels and foundations to spend 3 months in George Church’s Lab working on gene therapies for Parkinson’s and adrenoleukodystrophy.

Outside the lab, I'm President of the Cambridge Ageing Research Society, with over 1000 members, and co-founder of the Lunar Society, hosting monthly dinners across London, Boston and San Francisco

It’s already clear in retrospect that biology is and has been the perfect venture for me to take on. It’s been a space for me to get away from challenges in my personal life, has taken me across the world, and has no conceivable ceiling for success.

Recovering resilience in the aged brain could serve as a once-and-for-all pan-dementia therapy, and I will explore this next in a PhD. Currently, I’m interested in learning as much as possible as fast as possible.
